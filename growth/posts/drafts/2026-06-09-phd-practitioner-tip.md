TITLE: How I went from 4 hours per interview to 45 minutes (transcription workflow for qualitative PhDs)

BODY:
After transcribing about 30 hours of interviews manually in my first year, I overhauled my workflow. Here's what actually made a difference:

**1. AI draft first, then correct — don't transcribe from scratch**
Modern cloud STT (I use HumanLogs, others use Whisper locally or Gladia directly) gets you to a 95%+ draft in ~2 minutes per hour of audio. You're doing correction, not transcription. That's the biggest single lever.

**2. Audio-linked editor for correction**
The workflow-breaker is switching between VLC/QuickTime and a text document. Get an editor where you can click any word and hear that exact moment. I cut correction time in half just from this.

**3. 1.25× playback speed during review**
Since you have a draft, you're verifying — not dictating. Your ears process faster than your hands can type. Start at 1.25×, move to 1.5× once you're comfortable.

**4. 45-minute sessions only**
This sounds counterproductive but transcription accuracy (yours and your catching-AI-errors rate) falls off a cliff after an hour of continuous work. Three 45-min blocks beats one 3-hour session.

**5. Sort out data storage before you start**
If you have EU participants, sort your GDPR compliance before uploading anything anywhere. Undoing this later is a nightmare.
