If a transcript already exists (from another tool, a colleague, or a corpus you were given) import it instead of paying to transcribe it again. On the **New transcription** screen, switch from **Transcribe audio** to **Import a document**.

Importing consumes **no credits**: no audio is processed.

## Accepted formats

Plain text (`.txt`), Word (`.docx`), CSV, JSON, subtitles (`.srt`, `.vtt`), NVivo transcripts and MAXQDA transcripts.

## What happens to timing

Subtitle files carry real timestamps and keep them. The other formats have no timing, so timestamps are synthesised from the text: the editor then behaves exactly as it does elsewhere, but there is no recording behind them, playback is inactive and the timings are not evidence of anything.

If you have both the transcript and its recording, transcribing the audio gives you a document where every word is anchored to a real moment. Import is the right choice when the audio is gone, was never yours, or must not leave your institution.

## What you get

A normal document: editable, commentable, shareable, assignable to a study, and exportable in every supported format. That last point is what makes import useful beyond convenience, bring in a MAXQDA transcript, work on it here, hand it back as NVivo.
