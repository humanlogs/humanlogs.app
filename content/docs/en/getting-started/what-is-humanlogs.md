HumanLogs turns interview recordings into transcripts you can actually work with: an editor bound to the audio, speaker labels, real-time collaboration, and exports to the tools the rest of your analysis lives in.

It is built for research material, which is why privacy is a feature and not a footnote. Audio and transcripts can be end-to-end encrypted in your browser, the transcription providers we use apply zero-retention policies, and you choose whether your audio is processed in the EU or in the US.

## Who it is for

Anyone who records people talking and then has to read, correct, quote and analyse what was said: researchers, PhD students, journalists, podcasters, teachers.

## What it does today

- **Transcribe**: audio or video, 100+ languages, speaker detection, custom vocabulary. Roughly ten minutes of processing per hour of audio.
- **Correct**: an editor where clicking a word moves the audio there, so a first pass on a one-hour interview takes 30 to 45 minutes rather than an afternoon.
- **Work together**: share a document by email with three levels of access, edit at the same time, discuss in comments.
- **Export**: Word, PDF, text, NVivo, MAXQDA, CSV, SRT, WebVTT, JSON, and your audio back.

## Where it is heading

Transcription is the first stage of a longer journey. **Coding** is being built: [codebooks](/docs/coding/codebooks) already exist, applying codes to passages does not yet. **[Analysis](/docs/analysis/learn-more)** comes after it. Both are labelled as such throughout these docs, so you always know what has shipped.

Everything you do now survives that: transcripts, studies and codebooks carry forward.

## Open source

AGPL v3, and the same codebase runs the hosted service and any self-hosted install. If your institution cannot send recordings to a third party, see the [installation guide](/docs/self-hosting/installation-guide): with a local Whisper server, the recordings never leave your network.
