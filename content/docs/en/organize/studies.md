A study is a folder for one project, a thesis chapter, a field campaign, a series of interviews.

## Create one

**New study** from the home screen. Give it a name and pick a colour: the colour is what makes documents recognisable at a glance in the sidebar, and it is worth choosing deliberately if you run several projects at once.

Assign a document from its actions menu or from the sidebar. Documents with no study stay under **Unassigned**, a perfectly fine place for one-off recordings, and not something to feel bad about.

## Why bother

Beyond tidiness, a study is the unit the rest of the product hangs off:

- **Codebooks** are scoped to studies, all of them, or a selection, and a study has its own coding board for going through a corpus in one pass. See [Codebooks](/docs/coding/codebooks).
- **The sidebar can group documents by the codes** of a speaker codebook, which turns a code into a working view of the corpus.
- **Comparisons** between interviews will work on a study when analysis ships.

Grouping your interviews now costs a click each and saves the retrofit later.

## Finding a document

The sidebar has its own search field, filtering by name as you type. View settings control how documents are grouped, by study, or as one flat recent-first list; pick whichever matches how you think about your corpus.

Documents other people shared with you sit in their own section, with the owner's name, so your material and someone else's never get confused. See [Collaboration](/docs/organize/collaboration).

## Renaming and deleting

A study can be renamed at any time; its documents follow.

Deleting one asks what should happen to the documents inside it: **move them**, to another study, or to Unassigned, or **delete them too**, transcripts and audio included. Deleting is permanent, so [export](/docs/transcribe/export) anything you may still need before you confirm.
