Transcription is one of the most time-consuming parts of qualitative research, yet it's also one of the most under-optimised. Many researchers still follow a 1:4 ratio: one hour of audio takes four hours to transcribe. It doesn't have to be that way.

This guide covers the practical techniques we've seen work best for PhD students, academic researchers, and journalism teams.

## 1. Use AI-assisted transcription as a first draft

Modern speech-to-text engines like [Gladia](https://gladia.io) and ElevenLabs Scribe reach 95 - 98% accuracy on clean interview audio. That accuracy is good enough to turn a 60-minute interview into a correctable draft in about 2 minutes, rather than typing it from scratch.

The key word is *correctable*. You're not handing off the job to AI; you're reducing the mechanical burden so you can focus on meaning.

**What to look for in an AI transcription tool:**
- Speaker diarisation (labeling who said what)
- Timestamps at the word or sentence level
- No audio retention (critical for IRB compliance and GDPR)
- An editor built for correction, not just reading

## 2. Master the audio-linked editor

The single biggest time-saver after getting an AI draft is an editor where you can click any word and hear the audio from that exact moment. Traditional transcript review means tabbing between a media player and a text document, constantly losing your place.

With an audio-linked editor you can:
- Click a suspicious word → hear it immediately
- Correct it → keep moving
- Never lose synchronisation between the audio and the text

HumanLogs was built around this workflow: the editor highlights words as they play and lets you click to seek. Researchers who switch from a standard word processor + VLC setup typically cut correction time by 60 - 70%.

## 3. Set up keyboard shortcuts before you start

Stopping to reach for the mouse breaks your flow. Before starting a correction session, spend 5 minutes learning, or customising, your keyboard shortcuts:

| Action | Default shortcut |
|--------|-----------------|
| Play / pause | `Alt + Space` |
| Previous / next segment | `←` / `→` |
| Previous / next sentence | `Shift + ←` / `Shift + →` |
| Speed up playback (2×) | Hold `Ctrl` |
| Slow down playback (0.5×) | Hold `Alt` |
| Turbo playback (4×) | Hold `Alt + Ctrl` |

In HumanLogs, all shortcuts are configurable from your account settings so you can match the layout you already know from Audacity, Adobe Premiere, or any other tool.

## 4. Transcribe at 1.25 - 1.5× speed

Once you have an AI draft, you're doing *verification*, not dictation. You can listen faster than you can type. Playing audio at 1.25× speed while scanning the transcript for errors is usually faster than normal speed and catches more errors than silent reading alone.

Start at 1.25× and move to 1.5× once you're comfortable. Most people plateau around 1.5 - 1.75× for correction work.

## 5. Batch your review sessions

Transcription accuracy degrades when you're tired. Studies of human transcription show error rates rising sharply after 45 - 60 minutes of continuous work. The same applies to AI correction.

**Practical approach:** Work in 45-minute blocks, then take a proper break. You'll cover more ground in 3 × 45 min than in one unbroken 3-hour session, and your transcript will be cleaner.

## 6. Handle confidentiality from the start

If your research involves sensitive topics or identifiable participants, encryption shouldn't be an afterthought. Setting up end-to-end encryption before you upload audio means your data is protected at rest and in transit, not just during the upload.

1. **Record** your interview (we recommend [Riverside.fm](https://riverside.fm) or Zoom with local recording enabled)
